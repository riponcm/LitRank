Scholar abbreviates and truncates venue names, and a truncated name can match several real journals. An Unranked badge means the venue was not matched — never that it scored badly. A tool that quietly guesses is worse than one that admits the gap, because from the outside you cannot tell the two apart.

Questions people actually ask

Does LitRank show the Journal Impact Factor?
No. LitRank shows SJR quartiles — Scimago Journal Rank — which is a different metric on a different scale from a different provider. Journal Impact Factor is a Clarivate metric published in Journal Citation Reports, and its data cannot be redistributed in a free extension. If you specifically need JIF, LitRank is not the right tool.
Why does a paper show “Unranked”?
Because the venue could not be matched with confidence — not because it scored badly. Usually the venue is not a journal at all (preprints, theses and book chapters carry no quartile), or Google Scholar truncated the name past the point where it is still unique. Where more than one journal could fit, LitRank shows no badge rather than guessing. The badge tells you which case you are looking at.
Why do conference papers have no quartile?
Quartiles are assigned to journals, not to conference proceedings. Rather than invent a rank for a conference, LitRank shows its impact score and h-index instead.
Is my reading list stored in the cloud?
No. Everything stays in your browser's local storage. There is no account, no server and no sync service. You can export a project or save it to a folder on your own computer whenever you like, but nothing leaves your machine unless you ask it to.
Can I use it with Zotero, Mendeley or EndNote?
Yes. Export any project as BibTeX, CSV or JSON and import it. Records carry a DOI wherever one could be recovered, so your reference manager can complete anything Google Scholar did not show.
Does it work on Google Scholar author profiles?
Yes. Author profiles get the same per-publication badges, plus a summary of the loaded publications: how many are Q1, the median impact score, and the spread across quartiles. Scholar loads profiles in pages, so the summary always says how many publications it covers.
Does it slow Google Scholar down?
No. The ranking data is prepared once per page, off the critical path, and every lookup after that is instant.
Is LitRank free?
Yes, and it stays free. No paid tier, no trial, no advertising and no data sale. It is built by Matily, a non-profit software studio.
